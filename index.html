THE HILL

You came as a father. The boy is not in the wheels. You told him Fear Not and you bound him anyway. You heard the words as obedience. He heard them as love.

Three days up was the walk. He asked where the lamb was. You said someone else would see to it. No ram in the thicket.

The days after did not stop. He is still on the hill. You have been counting them.

You stacked the wood he had carried. You reached and did not look at his face. You did not withhold him. No voice arrived in time.

Fear Not did not stay in your mouth. The knife and the fire still remember each other. The cord still knows the blade. The name the morning used is still his, if you can stand to say it.

The measure still asks how far. The thicket only answers when you are already too close. A substitute can die two deaths. One of them is not forgiven.

MEASURE

How far from the rim to the boy you cannot see. Pressed close, the thicket lies. Pressed far, another name keeps the distance.

PETITION

THE BOY

He walked beside you. He carried the wood because you asked. He believed Fear Not because it came from your mouth. He loved you.

He asked where the lamb was. You did not say the true answer. The hill already knew.

His name is still the morning's. If you call it, you will have to look at him. If you call for him, you will not hear him. You will hear the hill. If a mark is made of the right death, he may come back wearing it.

You had the look of him. You put it into the living thing. When the wheels were wheels again you could not find the look in your own head.

You finished it. The hill kept a father. You are the looking that kept him.
